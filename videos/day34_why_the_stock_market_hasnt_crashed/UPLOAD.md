📈 Why just 20 companies now make up nearly half of the S&P 500's total value, and why the index has gone up in about 7 out of every 10 years.
💻 How the "Magnificent Seven" tech giants drove about two thirds of the market's gains in 2023 and grew to about 31% of the S&P 500.
💵 The real engine: money on autopilot. How automatic 401(k) enrollment (Pension Protection Act of 2006) and index funds buy stocks every payday, no matter the headlines.
📊 How the first index fund, launched on December 31, 1975, was laughed at, and then grew bigger than the most famous active fund in America.
🏢 Companies buying themselves: how stock buybacks push prices up, and why Hewlett-Packard spent $67 billion buying back its own shares.
🏦 Cheaper money: how the Federal Reserve's rate cuts since September 2024 added fuel to the market.
🫧 Expensive can stay expensive: Alan Greenspan's "irrational exuberance" speech in 1996, the dot-com bubble (Nasdaq up 600%, then down 78%), Japan's 1989 peak that took 34 years to beat, and Black Monday 1987.
⚠️ The 3 real warning signs to watch instead of scary headlines: how much of the market sits in a few companies, how expensive stocks are compared to profits, and whether paycheck money keeps flowing in.

This is not financial advice. It's just how the machine works.
