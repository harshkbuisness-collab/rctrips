It is important not to romanticize every loss-making company by saying that Amazon lost money too.

## CHAPTER 3: The dot-com crash
Warehouses, transport, labor, and technology still cost money. The economic bet is that the value of customer loyalty and higher order volume can justify the expenses. That bet worked differently across products and years, and it required constant operational improvement rather than one clever advertisement.

Profitability, cash flow, customer satisfaction, competitive positioning, and financing capacity all matter. Saying that a company is investing for the future is not a substitute for explaining how the investment might create value. Amazon's history gives us reasons to take patient strategy seriously and reasons to remain skeptical of anyone using its success as an excuse for endless losses.

## CHAPTER 6: Warehouses and criticism
Labor conditions, warehouse safety, delivery pressure, marketplace treatment, data practices, and market competition have all attracted criticism and legal scrutiny. A serious business documentary should treat these concerns as part of the operating model, not as a brief interruption before returning to the founder's genius. Efficiency can create consumer benefits while placing substantial demands on workers and suppliers.
