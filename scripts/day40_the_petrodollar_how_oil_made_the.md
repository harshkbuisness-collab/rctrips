## CHAPTER 4: The deal
[calm] When oil prices quadruple, the countries selling it collect a mountain of cash. Most of that cash was in dollars.
> VISUAL: D17 oil barrel pops in; E01 cash stack drops in; E01 cash stack slides in from the right; D01 dollar happy; A15 narrator point right

> VISUAL: E35 small house pops in; E03 money rain drops in; H17 price tag drops in "x4"; A36 narrator mind blown
That was Saudi Arabia in nineteen seventy-four. It needed somewhere safe and enormous to put all that money.

## CHAPTER 5: What it gives America
[calm] The first gift is demand. If every country needs dollars to buy oil, every country needs to keep a pile of dollars.
> VISUAL: E43 globe pops in; D17 oil barrel; E01 cash stack drops in; D01 dollar happy; A15 narrator point right
Countries keep those piles as reserves. A reserve currency is the money central banks hold for emergencies and trade.
> VISUAL: G10 central bank building pops in; E21 safe vault drops in; D01 dollar happy; A38 narrator teacher pointer
By the middle of the twentieth century, the dollar had become the world's main reserve currency. Oil helped keep it there.
> VISUAL: E27 crown pops in; D01 dollar happy; E25 calendar flips; A21 narrator thumbs up
> SCREEN: Wikipedia, Reserve currency | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_currency | highlight "the United States dollar had become the world's dominant reserve currency"
The dollar is not the first world money, by the way. Hundreds of years ago, the Spanish silver dollar played that role across Europe, Asia and the Americas.
