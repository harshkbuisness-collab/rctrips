## CHAPTER 3: Entry-level squeeze
Those numbers can move differently. A company may post a difficult-to-fill specialist role for months. Another company may hire quickly for many common positions without leaving advertisements up for long.

That's why the existence of vacancies doesn't prove that employers are desperate for everyone available to work. Hiring involves budgets, internal candidates, changing priorities, and imperfect communication. At the same time, telling applicants to stay positive while ignoring genuine labor-market weakness is unhelpful. A realistic strategy combines information about industry demand with targeted effort, networking, and skill development.

Businesses often prefer experienced workers when uncertainty rises. Teams under pressure may hesitate to spend months training someone new. Universities continue graduating students who need their first chance.

## CHAPTER 7: The final answer
She cannot force an employer's budget to reopen or prevent an industry downturn. The goal is to use evidence to prioritize effort rather than treating every rejection as a judgment on her future. Labor markets are systems of imperfect matching.
> VISUAL: A06_narrator_thinking slides left; E07_laptop drops; D13_chart_arrow_up draws; C09_worker_woman_neutral pops right; E09_newspaper walks in; zoom; red circle; yellow highlight
Understanding them can make the process less mysterious even when it remains frustrating. Employers also have responsibilities. Posting realistic requirements, giving timely information, and being honest about whether a position is funded can save applicants enormous time.

A business that complains it can't find skilled people should also ask whether its wages, training pathways, and selection methods make sense.
