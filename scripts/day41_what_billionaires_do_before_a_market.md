## CHAPTER 4: Move three: betting against the market
[serious] His name was Jesse Livermore. He was one of the most famous stock traders who ever lived.
> VISUAL: C13 retiree neutral walks in; F05 bg trading floor; E24 clock pops in; A18 narrator present right
Livermore did not just sell his stocks. He bet that prices would fall. This is called shorting, or selling short.
> VISUAL: E46 arrow down big pops in; H18 sign drops in "SHORT"; D14 chart arrow down drops in; A38 narrator teacher pointer
Here is how shorting works. You borrow a share worth one hundred dollars and sell it. Later, if the price falls to sixty, you buy it back, return it, and keep forty dollars.
> VISUAL: H15 paper note drops in "$100"; E46 arrow down big; H15 paper note drops in "$60"; E04 coin pile pops in; A38 narrator teacher pointer
But if the price goes up instead, your losses can keep growing with no limit. It is one of the riskiest moves in finance.
> VISUAL: E45 arrow up big drops in; E47 explosion pops in; E44 warning sign; A09 narrator scared
Livermore was famous for these bets. The day before the great San Francisco earthquake of nineteen oh six, he had a big short bet on a railroad stock. It made him two hundred fifty thousand dollars.
> VISUAL: E25 calendar flips "1906"; E47 explosion pops in; E41 car drops in; H17 price tag drops in "$250,000"; A36 narrator mind blown

## CHAPTER 5: The clues you can actually see

## CHAPTER 6: Why most people do the opposite
[calm] Big market swings usually start and end with a crowd. Frenzied buying in a bubble. Frenzied selling in a crash.
> VISUAL: C28 crowd small walks in; D29 bubble character pops in; E47 explosion drops in; A10 narrator panic run
> SCREEN: Wikipedia, Herd behavior | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herd_behavior | highlight "Large stock market trends often begin and end with periods of frenzied buying (bubbles) or selling (crashes)."
Economists call this herd behavior. Greed pulls people in near the top. Fear pushes them out near the bottom.
> VISUAL: G01 bull happy walks in; G02 bear angry walks in; E22 scale drops in; A38 narrator teacher pointer
> SCREEN: Wikipedia, Herd behavior | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herd_behavior | highlight "greed in the bubbles, fear in the crashes"
Regular investors join the rush, buying when everyone is excited and selling when everyone is scared.
> VISUAL: C28 crowd small walks in; E45 arrow up big; E46 arrow down big; A10 narrator panic run
> SCREEN: Wikipedia, Herd behavior | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herd_behavior | highlight "Individual investors join the crowd of others in a rush to get in or out of the market."
That is the exact opposite of what Buffett did in two thousand eight. He bought when the crowd was selling.

The billionaires had something most people do not. Spare cash, no need to sell, and the nerves to wait.

## CHAPTER 7: What it means for Kevin
[calm] Here is the first lesson. The rich do not win by guessing the crash. They win by being ready when it comes.
> VISUAL: C21 billionaire neutral; E21 safe vault pops in; H16 sticky note drops in "READY"; A18 narrator present right
For a regular person, being ready looks boring. An emergency fund, so a job loss does not force you to sell at the bottom.
> VISUAL: E21 safe vault drops in; E01 cash stack; H15 paper note drops in "EMERGENCY FUND"; A21 narrator thumbs up
Many money experts suggest a few months of living costs, kept somewhere safe and easy to reach. It is not exciting. But it is Kevin's version of Buffett's cash pile.
> VISUAL: E25 calendar flips; E21 safe vault; H17 price tag drops in "3-6 MONTHS"; H09 guy curly neutral; A02 narrator happy
The second lesson. Avoid borrowed money in the stock market. Livermore's story shows how fast leverage turns a win into a disaster.
