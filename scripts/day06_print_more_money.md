## CHAPTER 6: How America Prints Money
[calm] First, a surprise. The U.S. government doesn't just print money to pay its bills. When it needs money, it borrows, by selling Treasury bonds to investors.

The paper bills you carry are printed by the Bureau of Engraving and Printing. But most money today isn't paper at all. It's numbers in bank accounts.
> VISUAL: D19_money_printer drops in; E07_laptop pops in; D15_bank_building pops in; H18 sign pops in "MOSTLY DIGITAL"
> SCREEN: Bureau of Engraving and Printing | https://www.bep.gov/ | highlight "Bureau of Engraving and Printing"

## CHAPTER 7: The Final Answer
[calm] A growing economy needs some new money. If more bread is baked every year, you need a few more dollars to buy it, or prices would fall.
> VISUAL: E39_grocery_bag drops in; E39_grocery_bag pops in; E01_cash_stack pops in; D13_chart_arrow_up draws on; A38_narrator_teacher_pointer slides in from the left
That's why central banks aim for slow, steady inflation, about two percent a year. A little new money, matched by a little more stuff.
> VISUAL: G10_central_bank_building drops in; G04_percent_happy pops in "2%"; E22_scale pops in; E54_snail pops in
> SCREEN: Federal Reserve | https://www.federalreserve.gov/faqs/economy_14400.htm | highlight "2 percent"
Creating money can also help in a panic, like two thousand eight or twenty twenty, when people suddenly stop spending and banks stop lending.
> VISUAL: D16_bank_building_scared drops in; E44_warning_sign pops in; G10_central_bank_building pops in; E03_money_rain pours down
It breaks when three things happen together. The country stops producing enough. The government spends far more than it collects. And the printer is used to cover the gap, year after year.
> VISUAL: H16 sticky note pops in "1. LESS STUFF"; H16 sticky note pops in "2. HUGE DEFICITS"; H16 sticky note pops in "3. PRINTER ON"; E47_explosion drops in
> SCREEN: Wikipedia | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation | highlight "Hyperinflation"
When that happens, people stop trusting the money. They spend it as fast as they can, which pushes prices up even faster. That's when inflation becomes hyperinflation.

Here's the takeaway. Money is a promise. It's only worth something because people trust that it will still buy things tomorrow. Print too much, and the promise breaks.
