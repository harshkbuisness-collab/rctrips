## CHAPTER 3: The Golden Handcuffs
[calm] Mr. Collins refinanced his mortgage in twenty twenty-one. He locked in a fixed rate of two point eight percent for thirty years.

> VISUAL: C15_retiree_happy walks in from the right; H17 price tag pops in "LOW PAYMENT"; E23_hourglass pops in; E28_trophy drops in
But if he sells, that deal disappears. Any new home he buys would come with a new mortgage, at today's much higher rate.

## CHAPTER 4: Not Enough Homes
[calm] After the two thousand eight crash, builders went bust or got very careful. For about a decade, America built far fewer homes than it needed.

## CHAPTER 6: What Could Unlock It
[calm] The first fix is the simplest, and the slowest. Build more homes. Many more, of every kind, especially in the places where people want to live.
> VISUAL: H24_bricks_stack drops in; D09_house_happy pops in; H23_apartment_building pops in; E35_small_house pops in; A21_narrator_thumbs_up slides in from the left
Some cities have started changing their rules. Minneapolis ended single-family-only zoning in twenty eighteen. Austin cut its minimum lot size. Studies found rents in Minneapolis rose more slowly than in many similar cities.
> VISUAL: G13_map_usa_grey drops in; H22_book_open pops in; E20_trash_can pops in "OLD RULES"; D14_chart_arrow_down draws on
> SCREEN: Wikipedia | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis_2040 | highlight "Minneapolis 2040"

> VISUAL: E35_small_house drops in; H23_apartment_building pops in; D09_house_happy pops in; E29_ladder pops in
