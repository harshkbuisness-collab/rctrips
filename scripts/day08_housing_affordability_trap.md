## CHAPTER 2: The rate lock: why owners won't sell
Even moving to a similarly priced home could raise his monthly payment. So he stays. Thousands of households can make similar decisions for entirely reasonable reasons.
> VISUAL: A18_narrator_present_right slides left; D09_house_happy drops; D11_house_expensive draws; E35_small_house pops right; G21_landlord walks in; zoom; red circle; yellow highlight
Economists call this mortgage-rate lock-in. It reduces the number of homes entering the resale market. This matters because housing isn't an ordinary product.
> VISUAL: A38_narrator_teacher_pointer slides left; D11_house_expensive drops; E35_small_house draws; G21_landlord pops right; D09_house_happy walks in; zoom; red circle; yellow highlight
You can manufacture more shoes quickly when customers want them. You cannot produce a new neighborhood next week. Construction requires land, financing, labor, permits, roads, water connections, and approval.

Where people most want to live, land may be scarce or local rules may limit the number and type of homes that can be built. If demand rises in those places, supply often responds slowly. Higher prices should, in theory, encourage more building.

But builders can run into costs and restrictions long before they can deliver new homes. Imagine Maya looking at a neighborhood with excellent schools and reliable transit. She finds a small detached home on a large lot.

Residents may support affordable housing in general while opposing specific developments near their streets. Infrastructure can also be a real constraint. More homes require capacity for sewers, roads, and public services.
> VISUAL: A38_narrator_teacher_pointer slides left; E35_small_house drops; G21_landlord draws; D09_house_happy pops right; D11_house_expensive walks in; zoom; red circle; yellow highlight
The practical result is that places with the strongest demand can be among the hardest places to expand. Builders have their own economics. They must purchase land, pay interest on construction financing, hire contractors, and take the risk that buyers may disappear by the time a project is finished.

## CHAPTER 3: Builders and supply
Housing supply can therefore respond to expensive credit in the opposite direction from what frustrated buyers hope: fewer new projects, even though affordability is already poor. Let's calculate what a mortgage-rate change means using a hypothetical home. Suppose Maya wants to borrow three hundred thousand dollars on a thirty-year fixed mortgage.
> VISUAL: A15_narrator_point_right slides left; D11_house_expensive drops; E35_small_house draws; G21_landlord pops right; D09_house_happy walks in; zoom; red circle; yellow highlight
> SCREEN: Federal Reserve — Survey of Consumer Finances | https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/october-2023-changes-in-us-family-finances-from-2019-to-2022.htm | highlight exactly "net worth"
At a relatively low rate, the monthly principal-and-interest payment may feel manageable. At a significantly higher rate, the payment rises even though the borrowed amount is the same. Insurance, property taxes, maintenance, and repairs come on top.
> VISUAL: A18_narrator_present_right slides left; E35_small_house drops; G21_landlord draws; D09_house_happy pops right; D11_house_expensive walks in; zoom; red circle; yellow highlight
That is why housing affordability depends on more than the advertised sale price. People often see a listing marked down by a few thousand dollars and assume it's a bargain, but the financing terms can matter much more to the actual household budget. We also need to distinguish two groups who are sometimes blended into one statistic: potential buyers who cannot qualify for loans and people who can qualify but choose not to purchase.
> VISUAL: A38_narrator_teacher_pointer slides left; G21_landlord drops; D09_house_happy draws; D11_house_expensive pops right; E35_small_house walks in; zoom; red circle; yellow highlight
> SCREEN: FHFA — Lock-In Effect of Rising Mortgage Rates | https://www.fhfa.gov/research/papers/wp2403?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block | highlight exactly "increased home prices"
The first group is constrained by income, debt, credit, down payments, and lending standards. The second may be waiting because prices feel unattractive, employment is uncertain, or they expect rates to improve. Those groups respond differently to policy.
> VISUAL: A01_narrator_neutral slides left; D09_house_happy drops; D11_house_expensive draws; E35_small_house pops right; G21_landlord walks in; zoom; red circle; yellow highlight
A small price discount may help an almost-qualified buyer while doing nothing for someone whose earnings are far below the required level. Building more lower-cost homes may help one market, while improving access to jobs or transport may matter more in another. There is also a crucial difference between building houses and building useful communities.
> VISUAL: A06_narrator_thinking slides left; D11_house_expensive drops; E35_small_house draws; G21_landlord pops right; D09_house_happy walks in; zoom; red circle; yellow highlight
A subdivision with cheap lots but no access to jobs may not solve affordability for workers who need to commute to a major city. Density close to employment and transit can create options, but local planning must still consider schools, water, public space, and resilience. Housing policy isn't simply counting new roofs.

## CHAPTER 4: Zoning and why we don't build
It is creating places where households can live sustainably. When people debate a new apartment building or housing development, the question should be whether the benefits and costs are being measured realistically, not whether change is automatically good or bad. Finally, remember that a homeowner's financial gain can be a young buyer's obstacle.

## CHAPTER 5: Investors and the 'Bank of Mom and Dad'
People who already own property benefit when values rise. People trying to buy their first property face an increasingly expensive entry ticket. And someone who rents may spend years trying to save a deposit while rent itself consumes the money they need to save.
> VISUAL: A01_narrator_neutral slides left; E35_small_house drops; G21_landlord draws; D09_house_happy pops right; D11_house_expensive walks in; zoom; red circle; yellow highlight
But wait. If affordability is this strained, why don't prices simply collapse? Sometimes they do decline, particularly in markets with excess supply or weak local demand.

Housing is not one national market with one price. A city gaining well-paid jobs may behave very differently from a town losing residents. And when owners are reluctant to list, fewer homes compete for buyers.

## CHAPTER 6: What would actually fix it
A discount on one expensive property may help one buyer. It doesn't create the thousands of additional homes the city needs. Likewise, adding a few luxury apartments doesn't instantly make every neighborhood affordable, although additional supply can influence the broader market through chains of moving households and changing competition.
> VISUAL: A15_narrator_point_right slides left; D11_house_expensive drops; E35_small_house draws; G21_landlord pops right; D09_house_happy walks in; zoom; red circle; yellow highlight
Housing supply works over time, across locations and price segments. That makes simple national slogans much less useful than local data. A healthier housing system also requires thinking about the existing stock.
> VISUAL: A18_narrator_present_right slides left; D14_chart_arrow_down drops; C01_banker_neutral draws; G11_stock_exchange pops right; D13_chart_arrow_up walks in; zoom; red circle; yellow highlight
Some homes are underused because moving is costly. Others need repairs before they can be occupied. Rules around accessory dwelling units, conversions, and different building types may allow neighborhoods to adapt without starting every development from an empty field.

## CHAPTER 7: The final answer
Listing prices can differ from accepted offers. Mortgage-rate averages don't capture each borrower's fees, credit history, or loan structure. When someone tells Maya that the housing market is definitely improving or collapsing, she should ask which city, which property type, and which measure they're using.
