## CHAPTER 2: A bond is just an IOU
[calm] A bond is a loan. But instead of you borrowing from a bank, someone else borrows from you.
> VISUAL: H15 paper note drops in "IOU"; E01 cash stack slides in from the left; D15 bank building pops in; A38 narrator teacher pointer
Imagine your neighbor needs one thousand dollars. She writes a note. Lend me one thousand dollars, I will pay you thirty dollars every year, and I will give the thousand back in ten years.
> VISUAL: C09 worker woman neutral walks in; H15 paper note drops in "$1,000"; E25 calendar flips; E04 coin pile pops in; A02 narrator happy
That note is a bond. The yearly payment is called the coupon. The day you get your money back is called the maturity date.
> VISUAL: D24 bond document pops in; H16 sticky note drops in "COUPON"; H16 sticky note drops in "MATURITY"; A38 narrator teacher pointer
> SCREEN: Wikipedia, Bond (finance) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_(finance) | highlight "The issuer is obligated to repay the nominal amount on the maturity date."
The big difference from a stock is the end date. A stock can last forever. A bond has a set day when the loan is finished.
> VISUAL: G11 stock exchange pops in; D24 bond document drops in; E25 calendar flips; E23 hourglass; A15 narrator point right
> SCREEN: Wikipedia, Bond (finance) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_(finance) | highlight "bonds usually have a defined term, or maturity, after which the bond is redeemed"
Some bonds last a few years. Some last thirty. A few have even been sold with terms of fifty years or more.

That means a bond can be a promise that outlives the person who bought it.

## CHAPTER 3: The see-saw
[calm] Here is the one rule you need to remember about bonds. When interest rates go up, the price of old bonds goes down.
> VISUAL: E22 scale drops in; G05 percent angry pops in; D24 bond document; E46 arrow down big; A38 narrator teacher pointer
> SCREEN: Wikipedia, Bond vigilante | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_vigilante | highlight "In the bond market , prices move inversely to yields."
Think of it like a see-saw. Interest rates sit on one end. Bond prices sit on the other. When one goes up, the other goes down.
> VISUAL: E22 scale pops in; G05 percent angry drops in; D24 bond document; E45 arrow up big; E46 arrow down big; A17 narrator point up
Let's go back to your neighbor's note. It pays thirty dollars a year on one thousand dollars. That is three percent.
> VISUAL: H15 paper note drops in "3%"; E04 coin pile pops in; C09 worker woman neutral; A02 narrator happy
Now imagine that a year later, brand new bonds pay five percent. Fifty dollars a year on the same thousand.
> VISUAL: D24 bond document slides in from the right; H17 price tag drops in "5%"; E04 coin pile drops in; A04 narrator smug
If you try to sell your old three percent note, who would pay full price for it? Nobody. They can just buy a new one that pays more.
> VISUAL: C28 crowd small walks in; A14 narrator shrug; red X on H15; H15 paper note
So to sell it, you have to lower the price. Your thousand-dollar bond might only sell for eight or nine hundred.
> VISUAL: H17 price tag drops in "$850"; D02 dollar sad pops in; E46 arrow down big; A35 narrator sweating
The yearly payment is fixed. The market decides the price.
> VISUAL: D24 bond document pops in; E04 coin pile; G11 stock exchange slides in from the right; A38 narrator teacher pointer
> SCREEN: Wikipedia, Bond (finance) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_(finance) | highlight "The terms of the bond, such as the coupon, are fixed in advance and the price is determined by the market."
And the longer the bond, the bigger the swing. A bond that has thirty years left loses much more value than one that ends next year.
> VISUAL: E25 calendar flips "30 YEARS"; E46 arrow down big drops in; E25 calendar flips "1 YEAR"; A06 narrator thinking
The same see-saw works the other way. If rates fall, old bonds with higher payments become more valuable.
> VISUAL: G04 percent happy pops in; E45 arrow up big; D24 bond document; A02 narrator happy
This little see-saw sounds harmless. In twenty twenty-three, it took down one of the biggest banks in America. We will get there. First, back to Jordan.

## CHAPTER 8: What it means for your money
[calm] So here is the big idea. The bond market is where the price of money is set. Not by one person, but by millions of lenders every day.
> VISUAL: E43 globe pops in; C28 crowd small walks in; D24 bond document; H17 price tag drops in "PRICE OF MONEY"; A18 narrator present right
When lenders feel calm, borrowing gets cheaper. When they get nervous, borrowing gets expensive for everyone, from the government to Jordan.
> VISUAL: E22 scale pops in; G04 percent happy; G05 percent angry; G09 capitol building; H09 guy curly neutral
That is why the ten-year Treasury rate is worth watching. It moves your mortgage, your car loan, and even what businesses can afford to build.
> VISUAL: D09 house happy pops in; E41 car drops in; E33 factory slides in from the right; A38 narrator teacher pointer
It also explains your own savings. If you own a bond fund in your retirement account, its value goes down when rates go up, and back up when rates fall.
> VISUAL: D25 retirement document pops in; E22 scale; E46 arrow down big; E45 arrow up big; A06 narrator thinking
