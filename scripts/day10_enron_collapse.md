## CHAPTER 3: Counting Money From the Future
[calm] The rule was called mark-to-market accounting. In nineteen ninety-two, regulators allowed Enron to use it for its contracts.
> VISUAL: H22_book_open drops in "MARK-TO-MARKET"; G09_capitol_building pops in; E25_calendar pops in "1992"; A38_narrator_teacher_pointer slides in from the left
> SCREEN: Wikipedia | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark-to-market_accounting | highlight "Mark-to-market accounting"
Here's what it meant. When Enron signed a contract that would pay off over twenty years, it could count all the expected profit right away, on day one.
> VISUAL: H15_paper_note_blank drops in "20-YEAR CONTRACT"; E23_hourglass pops in; E02_money_bag pops in "TODAY"; A08_narrator_shocked slides in from the left
Imagine a lemonade stand that signs a deal to sell lemonade every summer for twenty years. It tells everyone it already made twenty years of profit, today.
> VISUAL: E40_coffee_cup drops in; E25_calendar pops in "20 SUMMERS"; E03_money_rain pours down; A04_narrator_smug slides in from the left
> SCREEN: Investor.gov, U.S. SEC | https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/researching-investments/how-read-10-k | highlight "10-K"
And who decided how much profit those contracts would make? Enron did. Its own estimates. Often very optimistic ones.
> VISUAL: C02_banker_greedy walks in from the right; D27_calculator_shocked pops in; E26_rocket drops in; E48_question_marks pop in
If a project later failed, the profit was already reported. So Enron had to keep signing bigger deals to show new profits every quarter.

## CHAPTER 4: The Hidden Rooms
[calm] Enron's chief financial officer was Andrew Fastow. His job was to make the company's money look good. He became very creative at it.

## CHAPTER 7: What Changed
[calm] In two thousand two, Andrew Fastow pleaded guilty to fraud. He was sentenced to six years in prison.
> VISUAL: H13_bowtie_man_smug walks in from the right; E55_handcuffs_chain drops in; E14_podium pops in; H17 price tag pops in "6 YEARS"
In two thousand six, a jury convicted Jeff Skilling and Ken Lay. Skilling was sentenced to twenty-four years, later reduced. Lay died of a heart attack before he could be sentenced.
> VISUAL: E14_podium drops in; E55_handcuffs_chain pops in; H17 price tag pops in "24 YEARS"; E25_calendar pops in "2006"
