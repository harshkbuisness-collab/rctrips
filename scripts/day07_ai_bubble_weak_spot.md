## CHAPTER 3: Who Pays the Bill?
[calm] Think about a new shopping mall. The builder spends millions on the building. But the mall only makes money if stores rent space, and shoppers actually buy things.
> VISUAL: A38_narrator_teacher_pointer slides in from the left; E34_skyscraper drops in "MALL"; E36_cart_full pops in; C28_crowd_small pops in
AI works the same way. Tech companies build data centers. Then they rent out the computing power, or sell AI tools, to businesses and people.
> VISUAL: E33_factory drops in; D23_ai_chip pops in; E07_laptop pops in; C28_crowd_small pops in; red arrow
Some companies charge a monthly subscription. Others charge businesses for every question their apps send to the AI. Either way, revenue has to come from somewhere.
> VISUAL: E07_laptop drops in; H17 price tag pops in "$20/month"; E08_phone pops in; E01_cash_stack pops in
For the math to work, those customers must pay enough, year after year, to cover the buildings, the chips, the electricity, and a profit on top.

## CHAPTER 4: The Last Time This Happened
[calm] In the late nineteen nineties, everyone was sure the internet would change the world. They were right.

> VISUAL: E07_laptop pops in; H18 sign drops in ".COM"; D13_chart_arrow_up draws on; E26_rocket pops in
So companies raced to build the internet's roads. Phone and cable companies laid huge amounts of fiber-optic cable under oceans and across America.
> VISUAL: E31_cargo_ship drops in; G13_map_usa_grey pops in; E43_globe pops in; E33_factory pops in
The star of that boom was Cisco, which sold the equipment that moved internet traffic. In March two thousand, it briefly became the most valuable company in the world.

Sound familiar? Cisco was the NVIDIA of its time. The company selling the shovels in a gold rush.

## CHAPTER 5: What's Different This Time
[calm] The big difference is who's spending. In the dot-com bubble, many companies had no profits at all. Today's biggest AI spenders are some of the most profitable companies ever.
> VISUAL: E34_skyscraper drops in; E02_money_bag pops in; E28_trophy pops in; A21_narrator_thumbs_up slides in from the left
> SCREEN: Microsoft Investor Relations | https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor | highlight "Investor Relations"
Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta earn huge amounts from software, ads and cloud services. Much of their AI spending comes from profits they already have.
> VISUAL: E34_skyscraper pops in; E07_laptop pops in; E01_cash_stack drops in; E21_safe_vault pops in
> SCREEN: Alphabet Investor Relations | https://abc.xyz/investor/ | highlight "Alphabet"
That's very different from two thousand. Back then, many companies burned through borrowed money and simply ran out.
> VISUAL: E25_calendar pops in "2000"; D28_debt_monster drops in; E19_fire pops in; E20_trash_can pops in
So if AI disappoints, these companies would likely survive. They'd just have spent a lot of money on buildings worth less than they hoped.
> VISUAL: E34_skyscraper pops in; E33_factory drops in; D14_chart_arrow_down draws on; A14_narrator_shrug slides in from the left
But here's the new danger. More and more of the spending is now done with borrowed money, and through deals where companies pay each other in circles.
> VISUAL: E44_warning_sign drops in; D28_debt_monster pops in; red circle arrows; A05_narrator_suspicious slides in from the left
For example, in twenty twenty-five, NVIDIA said it would invest up to one hundred billion dollars in OpenAI. OpenAI, in turn, plans to buy huge amounts of NVIDIA chips.
> VISUAL: D23_ai_chip drops in "NVIDIA"; C17_robot_neutral pops in "OpenAI"; E02_money_bag pops in; red arrow from NVIDIA to OpenAI; red arrow back
> SCREEN: Wikipedia | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI | highlight "OpenAI"
When a seller helps fund its own customers, sales can look stronger than the real demand underneath. Analysts call this circular deals.
> VISUAL: A38_narrator_teacher_pointer slides in from the left; E02_money_bag pops in; red circle; H18 sign drops in "CIRCULAR DEALS"
It's a bit like a car dealer lending you the money to buy his car. The sale counts. But if you can't pay him back, he loses twice.
> VISUAL: E41_car drops in; C01_banker_neutral walks in from the right; E02_money_bag pops in; D14_chart_arrow_down draws on
Some data centers are also being paid for with big loans and bonds. Debt is fine when revenue arrives on time. It's dangerous when it doesn't.
> VISUAL: D24_bond_document drops in; D28_debt_monster pops in; E24_clock pops in; E44_warning_sign pops in
> SCREEN: Investor.gov, U.S. SEC | https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/bonds-or-fixed-income-products/bonds | highlight "Bonds"
And chips get old fast. A top AI chip may be outdated in just a few years, when a faster one comes out. The building lasts decades. The chips inside it don't.
> VISUAL: D23_ai_chip pops in; E23_hourglass drops in; D23_ai_chip slides in from the right; E20_trash_can pops in
So the bill doesn't just need to be paid once. It needs to be paid again and again, every time the chips are replaced.
> VISUAL: D27_calculator_shocked drops in; H15_paper_note_blank pops in "BILL"; H15_paper_note_blank pops in "BILL"; H15_paper_note_blank pops in "BILL"
Add it all up, and the AI industry needs to grow its revenue many times over, in just a few years, to make today's spending look smart. It's possible. But it's a very big ask.

## CHAPTER 7: Protecting Your Bet
[calm] First, know what you own. Open your retirement account and look at your funds. Many people are surprised by how much is in a few big tech companies.
> VISUAL: D25_retirement_document drops in; E16_magnifying_glass pops in; E34_skyscraper pops in; A39_narrator_magnifying slides in from the left
> SCREEN: Investor.gov, U.S. SEC | https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/save-and-invest/diversify-your-investments | highlight "diversify"
Second, spread your bets. Owning different kinds of investments, like bonds, smaller companies, or companies outside the U.S., means one bubble can't sink everything.
> VISUAL: H19_box_closed drops in; D24_bond_document pops in; E43_globe pops in; E04_coin_pile pops in; E22_scale pops in
Third, don't chase. When everyone around you says a stock can only go up, that's usually when it's most expensive.
> VISUAL: A48_narrator_stop_hand slides in from the left; E26_rocket pops in; C28_crowd_small drops in; E44_warning_sign pops in
> SCREEN: Investor.gov, U.S. SEC | https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/asset-allocation | highlight "Asset Allocation"
And fourth, if your job depends on one boom, keep extra savings. Rosa is now putting a little aside every month, just in case the overtime stops.
