## CHAPTER 5: Two more engines
[calm] The second engine is companies buying their own stock. This is called a share buyback.
> VISUAL: E34 skyscraper pops in; E01 cash stack slides in from the right; A38 narrator teacher pointer; H18 sign drops in "BUYBACK"
> SCREEN: Wikipedia, Share repurchase | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_repurchase | highlight "is the reacquisition by a company of its own shares"
Imagine a pizza cut into ten slices. If the owner eats two slices, each remaining slice is now a bigger share of the pizza.
> VISUAL: H19 box closed pops in; H20 box open drops in; E04 coin pile; A02 narrator happy
When a company buys back shares, every remaining share owns a bigger piece of the profits. So each share can be worth more.
> VISUAL: E02 money bag drops in; D13 chart arrow up pops in; C21 billionaire neutral walks in; A04 narrator smug
American companies started doing this on a huge scale in the late twentieth century, and they never really stopped.
> VISUAL: E25 calendar flips; E34 skyscraper slides in from the right; E03 money rain; A06 narrator thinking

## CHAPTER 7: What it means for Maya
[calm] First, the good news. Maya's autopilot is the same thing that makes the market hard to knock down. Steady buyers are a cushion.
> VISUAL: C11 worker woman happy pops in; E21 safe vault drops in; H27 heart pops in; A21 narrator thumbs up
Second, the honest news. A market carried by a few giants is fragile in a new way. If those giants stumble, almost everyone's retirement stumbles with them.
> VISUAL: E34 skyscraper pops in; E30 broken bridge drops in; C14 retiree worried; A35 narrator sweating
Maya does not own seven companies on purpose. But through her fund, a big piece of her savings depends on them.
> VISUAL: C09 worker woman neutral; E22 scale drops in; H16 sticky note drops in "7"; A06 narrator thinking
There is one more quiet change coming. Many retirement funds slowly move money out of stocks and into bonds as people get closer to retiring.
> VISUAL: C13 retiree neutral walks in; D24 bond document drops in; E46 arrow down big; A15 narrator point right
> SCREEN: Wikipedia, 401(k) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/401(k) | highlight "automatically shifting investments from stocks to bonds based on time to planned retirement date"
A bond is a loan you give to a government or company. It pays you interest, and it usually moves less than stocks.
> VISUAL: D24 bond document pops in; G09 capitol building slides in from the right; E04 coin pile; A38 narrator teacher pointer
So the same autopilot that buys stocks for young workers like Maya slowly sells stocks for older workers. As America ages, that balance could shift.
> VISUAL: C09 worker woman neutral; C14 retiree worried walks in; E22 scale drops in; A06 narrator thinking
So if you want to watch for real warning signs, do not watch scary headlines. Watch three simple things.
> VISUAL: E16 magnifying glass pops in; A39 narrator magnifying; E44 warning sign; H18 sign drops in "3 SIGNS"

One. How much of the market sits in just a few companies. Two. How expensive stocks are compared to profits. Three. Whether the paycheck money keeps flowing, or people start pulling it out.
