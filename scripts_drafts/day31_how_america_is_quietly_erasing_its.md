## CHAPTER 5: Keeping rates low
A negative real interest rate sounds technical, but the idea is simple. If a bond pays two percent while prices increase four percent, the buyer loses purchasing power.
> VISUAL: D24 slides in; G09 drops in; E01 pops in; D01 walks in; E25 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.
Governments have historically benefited when rates stay below inflation. The hidden cost lands on savers and on institutions that hold low-yield securities.
> VISUAL: G09 slides in; E01 drops in; D01 pops in; E25 walks in; D28 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.
Some governments historically limited financial choices or maintained policies that kept interest rates unusually low compared with price growth.
