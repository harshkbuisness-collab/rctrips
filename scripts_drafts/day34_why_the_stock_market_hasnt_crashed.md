## CHAPTER 2: Easy answer 1, "the economy is fine"
The strongest straightforward explanation is that many companies remain profitable and investors look beyond the present quarter. Employment, consumer spending, earnings and financing costs do not always weaken at the same moment.

A market can price in trouble early and rise when actual results are less severe than feared. A slowdown can hit businesses unevenly.
> VISUAL: D13 slides in; D14 drops in; G01 pops in; G02 walks in; A06 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.
A company selling essential services might keep growing while a cyclical manufacturer struggles. A multinational may earn money overseas even when domestic consumers are careful.

An index combines these fortunes into one line. Saying the economy is fine because the index rose is as misleading as saying every listed company is doomed because one industry suffers.

## CHAPTER 4: The real engines
Automatic payroll investing puts money into retirement accounts regularly. Companies sometimes buy back shares.

> VISUAL: G11 slides in; D13 drops in; D14 pops in; G01 walks in; G02 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.
Automatic investments are predictable in timing but not in market impact. Every buyer has a seller, and prices adjust to what both sides accept.
> VISUAL: D13 slides in; D14 drops in; G01 pops in; G02 walks in; A06 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.

An index chart is quoted in dollars. Over time inflation can push nominal revenue and nominal share prices higher without creating the same increase in purchasing power.
