## CHAPTER 2: Who sells bonds
Governments sell bonds to help fund budgets. Companies sell bonds for factories, acquisitions or refinancing.
> VISUAL: D24 slides in; D13 drops in; D14 pops in; D09 walks in; D15 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.
> SCREEN: SIFMA | https://www.sifma.org/research/statistics/us-fixed-income-securities-statistics | highlight exact page text "US Fixed Income Securities Statistics"
Investors buy them for income, stability or liquidity. Unlike a stockholder, the bondholder usually has a contractual claim, not ownership in the business.
> VISUAL: D13 slides in; D14 drops in; D09 pops in; D15 walks in; E22 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.
The important questions are whether payments arrive and how much the security is worth today. A company issuing debt is not the same as selling stock.
> VISUAL: D14 slides in; D09 drops in; D15 pops in; E22 walks in; C01 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.
Stockholders share ownership and receive what remains after obligations are paid. Bondholders have specified promises, though those promises can still be broken.
> VISUAL: D09 slides in; D15 drops in; E22 pops in; C01 walks in; A38 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.

## CHAPTER 3: The see-saw
Consider two otherwise identical bonds. One old bond pays three dollars a year per hundred lent.
> VISUAL: D24 slides in; D13 drops in; D14 pops in; D09 walks in; D15 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.
New bonds start paying five. The old payment no longer looks attractive.
> VISUAL: D13 slides in; D14 drops in; D09 pops in; D15 walks in; E22 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.
Its resale price generally falls until buyers can earn a competitive yield. Bond prices and yields often move in opposite directions.
> VISUAL: D14 slides in; D09 drops in; D15 pops in; E22 walks in; C01 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.
Suppose a bond promises three dollars a year on a hundred-dollar face value.
> VISUAL: D09 slides in; D15 drops in; E22 pops in; C01 walks in; A38 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.

## CHAPTER 4: The 10-year Treasury

## CHAPTER 7: Bond vigilantes
So here are three questions: who owes the payment, when will it arrive, and what happens if market yields change before then?

Those questions matter more than sensational claims that bonds secretly control everything. Understanding a bond turns many financial headlines into understandable cause and effect.
> VISUAL: A38 slides in; D24 drops in; D13 pops in; D14 walks in; D09 draws in; red circle; zoom; yellow highlight.
