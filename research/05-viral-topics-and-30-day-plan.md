## 2. How the winning scripts are built

**The first 10 seconds always do one of these:**
- **A shocking number or contradiction.** "58% more sellers than buyers… and prices still went up." / "Worth more than Coca-Cola, McDonald's, Disney, Nike and Starbucks combined."
- **The whole story in one line.** "He lied about $1 stocks, made millions, and served only 22 months."
- **Personal stakes.** "Who's buying it? You. Your retirement account."
- **An open loop.** "But before we get to the rule…", "and it gets pretty ugly."

**Three structures that keep winning:**
1. **The story** (Crayon Capital, Casual Economics, 12–20 min):
   - Cold open with the ending.
   - Origin.
   - Rise, with a montage of excess.
   - The crack nobody noticed.
   - The collapse.
   - Aftermath (who paid, who got away).
   - A one-line moral.
   - Lots of short character dialogue.
2. **The investigation** (Casual Finance, 13–17 min):
   - A paradox in the news.
   - The easy explanations, shown to be wrong one by one.
   - The real hidden cause: a buyer, a rule or a mechanism.
   - One everyday analogy character.
   - Sponsor at about 40–50%, *before* the payoff.
   - "What this means for your mortgage, your stocks, your job."
   - "The media won't cover it this way because it isn't clickable."
   - A subscribe ask built into the argument.
   - A thesis line at the end.
3. **The list** (EverythingProfessor, Explains 101, 12–20 min):
   - "Section 1… section 5", or "every X explained", each part 1–3 minutes.
   - Easy to binge, easy to make.

**Humour:**
- A joke or pop-culture comparison every 15–30 seconds: a sports trade, a GTA trailer, LinkedIn, Hinge, "the Oklahoma drill".
- Self-roasting ("which explains a lot about my brokerage account").
- Real numbers with a punchline right after.

**Length:** the winners are mostly **12–17 minutes**. The very short "bananas" videos (2–9 minutes) only work because of a strong running gag.

**Titles:**
- Patterns: "… Explained", "The Real Story of…", "…Is Worse Than You Think", "Nobody Can…", "Why…".
- Never the answer in the title.
