**How to read the labels below**

## 0. The most important finding (read this first)

**These are not AI-cinematic videos.** They are **hand-drawn, stick-figure "doodle explainers" on a plain white background**, mixed with **real charts, news screenshots, company logos and short clips**. There is no 3D, no realistic AI imagery, no camera movement in the usual sense and no image-to-video animation that I could find. [Observed, high confidence]

What makes them work is mainly:
1. **The script.** It is well researched, sarcastic and funny, with a strong "this affects *you*" angle.
2. **Very frequent small visual changes.** A new drawing, label, speech bubble or pose appears every **1–3 seconds**, and each one lines up with what the narrator is saying at that moment.

Production budget goes into **writing and research**, not image generation. [Estimate, high confidence]

**What this means for your plan:** Google Flow's cinematic images and video clips are the wrong tool for copying *this* style. The good news is that the style is **cheap to produce**, and well suited to a reusable library of characters plus some light automation. Section 7 has the details.

### Patterns [Estimate, medium–high confidence]

### Thumbnail analysis

*Source: a PDF screenshot of the channel's "Popular" Videos tab showing **33 thumbnails** (supplied 17 Oct 2026). The images are small, so fine details like exact fonts and colour values are estimates.*

**The winning formula** [Observed in about 22 of 33 thumbnails, including **all of the top 8**]

```
+------------------------------------------------+
| Short Statement.        [ real chart, line   ] |
| (black serif, 2 lines)  [ shoots up in RED,  ] |
|                         [ red circles/arrows,] |
|   (character)           [ "2008" vs "2026"   ] |
|  worried / blank        [ in red handwriting ] |
+------------------------------------------------+
            plain WHITE background
```

1. **Text, top-left.** It is 2–4 words in **bold black serif type** (newspaper-like), on two lines and ending with a full stop. It reads as a calm, ominous **statement**, not a question.
   - Examples: "This Should've Crashed.", "It's Happening Again.", "Nobody Can Stop It.", "It's A Trap.", "It's Just Starting.", "They're Selling Garbage.", "Nobody's Reacting.", "Guess Who Paid.", "We Can't Stop It.", "Open Your Eyes.", "Nobody's Paying Attention.", "Everyone's Missing This."
   - **The text never repeats the title.** It adds a second emotional layer: title = topic, thumbnail = feeling.
   - The vague subject ("It", "Nobody", "They") creates curiosity because the viewer must click to learn what "it" is.
   - Red is used for just one word occasionally ("Are We **Cooked.**", "We're **F\*\*\*D.**").
2. **A real data chart** on the right half: a screenshot of an actual financial chart.
   - The line usually **spikes up (or crashes) in red at the far right end**, meaning "now".
   - **Hand-drawn red annotations** sit on top: circles on peaks, arrows, and **year labels comparing a past crisis to today** ("2008" vs "2026", "1986" vs "2026", "2007–2008" vs "2026").
   - This says "it's happening again" without words.
   - Sometimes the chart is drawn **inside a cartoon frame** (an easel, a green chalkboard or a newspaper) so it feels part of the doodle world.
3. **One small character, bottom-left or centre**, with a **worried, blank or smug face**.
   - The recurring cast: the narrator stick figure; a **green USA-map character with big eyes** (the channel's real mascot, in about 10 thumbnails); a grey robot for AI topics; a top-hat banker; a politician-at-podium caricature; a Statue of Liberty.
   - The character is small (about 15–25% of the frame). **The data is the star, the character gives the emotion.**
4. **Lots of white space**, black linework, and **red as the only strong colour** (plus the green map). The result is very readable at phone size and stands out against the busy, photo-heavy thumbnails of other finance channels.

**Exceptions that performed worse** [Observed; time is a confounding factor]
- These broke the formula and sit lower on the popular list:
  - Full-colour illustrated scenes: crypto magician on stage (589K), "Something Feels Off" prize wheel (560K), Statue of Liberty drowning (120K).
  - A busy four-quadrant collage: "Every Type of Investor" (192K).
  - No-text doodles: "Dumbest Investors" (237K), "Stock Market is BROKEN" (116K).
- Most of these are **older**, from before the channel had an audience. So "the formula wins" is likely but **not proven** [Medium confidence].
- The formula clearly became the **standard** for every recent upload.

**How they are probably made** [Estimate]
- A simple layered composite in **Photoshop, Canva or similar**: a library character PNG + a chart screenshot + hand-drawn red marks + a serif font.
- Nothing looks AI-generated.
- The same **template** is reused every week, which makes the channel instantly recognisable in the feed.

**Branding seen on the page**
- **Avatar:** the narrator's face.
- **Banner:** a hand-drawn NYC skyline with "Subscribe" in handwriting.
- **Bio:** "i make finance videos".
- The channel has **Join** (memberships) and **Community** tabs enabled.

### Our original thumbnail system (proposal)

Keep the **principles**, change the **look**, so we are not a copy:

| Element | Casual Finance | Ours (proposal) |
|---|---|---|
| Statement text | Black serif, 2–4 words, full stop | A different typeface (e.g. a heavy condensed sans-serif), still 2–4 words and never repeating the title |
| Accent colour | Red only | Pick **one** different signature colour (e.g. orange or electric blue) for highlights and chart annotations |
| Mascot | Green USA map with eyes | Our own recurring mascot, e.g. a worried dollar bill or a piggy bank (to be designed) |
| Chart | Real chart, red "2008 vs 2026" labels | Real chart, our accent colour, our own annotation style (e.g. a highlighter box instead of circles) |
| Layout | Text top-left, character bottom-left, chart right | A mirrored or different fixed grid, used consistently |

**Thumbnail checklist for every video**
1. One chart that shows the story at a glance.
2. One emotional character, small.
3. One 2–4 word statement that adds to the title rather than repeating it.
4. One accent colour.
5. White space.
6. It must read clearly at phone size (test at 160 px wide).

**Automation:** once the template exists, I can produce two or three text and chart variants per video for A/B testing (YouTube's "Test & Compare" feature). Choosing the final one stays a human decision.

### Visual language [Observed]

- **Background:** plain white almost all the time. Occasionally there's a simple coloured room scene (a sofa and lamp), a green chalkboard, a cork board or a black "title card" with a glowing logo.
- **Characters:** hand-drawn stick figures with round flesh-coloured heads, black hair scribbles and thick black outlines. There is a recurring "narrator" figure with spiky black hair. Other characters get one identifying detail: a microphone, sunglasses, a grey hoodie, a bow tie, a tie, a ponytail.
- **Things become characters:** a 401k document with eyes, Japan's map with eyes and sunglasses, a box labelled "SpaceX Business". This is a big source of the humour.
- **Real material dropped in:** charts (Investing.com, the Financial Times, yield curves), news headlines with a red hand-drawn circle, company logos, a clip of a well-known investor, a GTA VI cover and memes ("Panik").
- **Handwritten text:** a hand-lettered font for speech bubbles, labels ("totally not annoying street interviewer") and title cards ("BREAKING NEWS!!!", "SUBSCRIBE!").
- **Colour:** mostly black and white. Colour is used sparingly for emphasis: green money, red arrows and circles, brand colours.
- **"Animation":** characters **swap between still poses** (for example arms down, then arms raised). There is **no smooth movement**. Objects simply appear (pop-in) or swap. I saw no zooming, panning or depth effects in the samples. [Observed in samples; I can't rule out an occasional zoom elsewhere]
- **Charts:** some are **built from scratch**. In the SpaceX opener, a bar chart grows step by step as each comparison is spoken. Others are screenshots.
- **Transitions:** almost all are **hard cuts**. Chapter breaks use a black or logo title card with a music sting.

**Likely made by hand vs. AI** [Estimate, medium confidence]
- The drawings look hand-drawn on a tablet. The line wobble is consistent and the style matches perfectly across videos.
- Nothing visibly looks AI-generated.
- The "animation" is ordinary editing: placing still images on a timeline and timing them to the voice.

**Tools I cannot verify:** something like After Effects, Premiere, CapCut or DaVinci Resolve could all produce this.

## 4. How these videos are realistically produced [Estimate]

1. **Research and script** (the creator says this takes "20+ hours" in the SpaceX sponsor read [Verified]).
2. **Record the narration**, then edit it tight (remove breaths and mistakes).
3. **Draw** characters and props on a tablet. A **library** of recurring characters and poses is reused across videos.
4. **Collect** charts, screenshots, logos and clips.
5. **Edit:** lay the narration down first, then place images on the timeline and time each one to a word.
6. Add title cards, music stings and loudness mastering, then export.

## 5. Asset requirements for our version

**Reusable library (made once, used in every video)**
- **Narrator character:** about 15 poses (neutral, pointing, shrug, arms up, thinking, facepalm, running, sitting, holding cash, holding phone) × 6 expressions (happy, sad, shocked, suspicious, smug, panicked).
- **4–6 supporting characters**, each with 5–8 poses: investor, banker, politician, "average person", interviewer, kid.
- **Object-characters:** a money bill, a house, a piggy bank, a 401k document, a stock chart, a country map. Each needs 2–3 expressions.
- **Props:** rocket, box, chalkboard, cork board, trash can, newspaper, phone, laptop, briefcase, arrows, circles, speech bubbles, title-card templates.
- **Audio:** our own music stings (3–5), a background bed (1–2), and 10–20 sound effects (pop, whoosh, cash register, record scratch).
- **Templates:** bar chart, line chart, headline-callout frame, chapter card, end card.

**Made per video (cannot be prepared in advance)**
- Custom charts with that video's data.
- Screenshots of news and filings.
- 20–40 one-off gag drawings for that script's jokes.
- The voiceover.

## 6. Voice strategy

**Recommendation: Option B (you supply the voiceover) as the main route.** Keep a free local voice only as a backup for drafts.

*Why:* this style depends heavily on **comic timing and sarcasm**. The best paid voices (or your own recorded voice) handle that much better than free models, and the voice is what viewers hear for 15 minutes.

**Free local options** [Licences must be re-checked before any commercial use]

| Model | Quality | Commercial use | Runs without a graphics card? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kokoro-82M** | Good, a bit flat | Yes (Apache 2.0) | Yes, fast |
| **Chatterbox (Resemble AI)** | Very good, some emotion control | Yes (MIT) | Slow without a graphics card |
| F5-TTS | Very good | **No** (weights are non-commercial) | Slow |
| XTTS-v2 (Coqui) | Good | **No** (non-commercial licence) | Slow |

**Never clone a real creator's voice**, including the reference narrator's.

**Option B workflow (no regeneration needed)**
1. You send the finished audio (WAV, or a high-quality MP3).
2. I run a free speech-to-text tool (e.g. Whisper) to get the **timing of every word**.
3. Each script line becomes a scene with start and end times, so images can be placed on exact words.

The one-time install (Whisper, running on the computer's main processor) is the single tool I'd need.

## 7. Critique of the "asset-first" plan, and a better version

**What's right about it:** a reusable character library is exactly how this style stays consistent and cheap.

**Weaknesses**
1. **About half the visuals are joke- and data-specific.** You can't generate them before the script is final, so "collect everything first" would mean wasted images.
2. **Google Flow's strength (cinematic video) isn't needed.** Image-to-video, camera moves and 3D add cost and inconsistency, and this style doesn't use them.
3. **AI image tools struggle with exact consistency** of simple line-art characters (line thickness, head size, hair). Small drift is very noticeable on a white background.

**Better: "Script-first, library-backed"**
1. Write the script.
2. Create a **beat sheet**: one visual per sentence or phrase, about one every 2 seconds.
3. Tag each beat as **library** (reuse), **template** (chart or headline) or **new** (generate or draw).
4. Generate only the "new" items. Use one fixed **style-reference sheet** and edit any off-model results.
5. Add the voiceover, take the word timings, then assemble.

**Assets: generate vs. make in the edit**

| Generate or draw outside the edit | Make in the edit |
|---|---|
| Characters and poses; object-characters; gag drawings; props | Charts and title cards (from templates); speech bubbles and labels; arrows and circles; headline call-outs; logos (official press kits) |

**Getting AI to match a hand-drawn look**
- Use a strong style prompt ("thick black marker stick-figure doodle, white background, flat").
- Add a reference image, and remove the background afterwards.
- **Test this in the proof of concept.** If drift is bad, the fallback is to hire a freelance illustrator for the **one-time** library (often a few hundred dollars; get quotes), then use AI only for one-off gags.

**Folder structure**

```
channel/
  library/
    characters/narrator/   narrator_point_happy.png ...
    characters/<name>/
    objects/               house_sad.png ...
    props/
    templates/             chart_bar.*, chapter_card.*, headline_frame.*
    audio/music/  audio/sfx/
    style_reference/       style_sheet.png, prompts.txt
  videos/
    2026-11_<slug>/
      01_script/   script.md, beat_sheet.csv
      02_assets/   new/ screenshots/ charts/
      03_voice/    voiceover.wav, word_timings.json
      04_project/
      05_export/   final.mp4, thumbnail.png
      SOURCES.md   (links for every screenshot, clip, data point)
```

**Naming rule:** `subject_pose_expression_v1.png`, all lowercase, no spaces.

## 9. Costs, risks and limitations

**Running costs** [Estimate: check current prices]
- Image AI: about $0–$20/month (Google AI plan).
- Voice: about $0–$22/month.
- Software: $0.
- Optional illustrator for the library: one-time.
- **Biggest hidden cost: time.** About 10–20 hours of research and writing per 15-minute video, even with AI help.

**Rendering**
- Still-image videos are light. A normal laptop renders a 15-minute 1080p video in minutes.
- This cloud session can also render, but files have to be uploaded and downloaded. That is the 30 MB-split hassle you just saw.

**Copyright and policy risks**
- **News screenshots, charts, logos and clips of other creators or podcasts:** these usually rely on *fair use* (commentary). That's a defence, not a guarantee. Keep clips short and directly commented on, and log sources in `SOURCES.md`.
- **Film posters, game art, memes:** higher risk. Use sparingly or redraw them.
- **Don't copy** Casual Finance's characters, name pattern or exact layouts. Make our own narrator design and our own visual signature (for example, one accent colour).
- **YouTube "inauthentic / mass-produced content" policy** (monetisation): templated AI-voice videos can be demonetised. Original research, a recognisable voice and real commentary are the protection.
- **AI disclosure:** YouTube requires a label for *realistic* synthetic content. Cartoon doodles generally don't need one, but re-check the rule when uploading.
- **Finance content:** add a "not financial advice" note. Fact-check every number, because a wrong figure damages a channel built on credibility.

**Quality risks**
- AI line-art drift.
- A flat-sounding AI voice killing the jokes.
- Too few visual changes (a static feel).
- Over-repeating library poses.

## 10. Implementation roadmap

1. **Decide our angle:** niche, name and narrator look. *Needs you.*
2. **Proof of concept (30–60 s):**
   - **Script:** I write about 120–150 words on one topic you pick, in our own voice.
   - **Beat sheet:** about 25 beats. Target 15–20 images: about 10 library-style (narrator in 5 poses, 1 supporting character, 2 object-characters, 2 props) and about 6 one-off (1 data chart I build, 1 headline call-out, about 4 gags).
   - **You generate** the images in Google Flow from my prompt sheet, plus the voiceover.
   - **I assemble** it automatically: word timings → placement → simple pop-ins → music sting → −14 LUFS → 1080p MP4.
   - **We measure:** your hours, AI cost, how consistent the characters look, and sync quality.
3. **Review:** if AI character consistency fails, switch the library to an illustrator.
4. **Build the full library** (about 80–120 images).
5. **Turn the assembly into a reusable tool:** beat sheet in, video out.
6. **First full video** (8–10 min to start), then a weekly rhythm. Add Shorts cut from long videos.
